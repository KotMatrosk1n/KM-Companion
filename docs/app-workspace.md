# App workspace

The app provides five main sections: Calculator, Dex, Encounters, Trainers, and Route Items. Settings opens its own page from the header. Team & Boxes opens from the bottom of the Calculator page. The live calculator stays mounted when switching sections, preserving the matchup and each section's position.

During startup, the original calculator markup stays invisible until the workspace, profile, and initial tab are assembled. The desktop window waits for both page load and first paint before appearing. If workspace initialization fails, the startup surface displays an error instead of exposing the unfinished layout.

The Theme dropdown offers four identities based on [KM Editor's palettes](https://github.com/KotMatrosk1n/KM-Editor/blob/master/apps/desktop/src/styles.css): Classic (navy and indigo), Renegade (charcoal and crimson), Royal (ivory and gold), and Sovereign (obsidian and amber). Classic is the default. A selection applies immediately and is restored before startup content appears. Tabs, selected rows, fields, menus, notes, and dialogs share the theme, with separate keyboard focus indicators. Speed borders and damage colors retain their battle meanings.

## Calculator

Player and Trainer editors sit beside a damage comparison. The desktop layout fits both populated teams, doubles slot cards, and all four moves without nested scrolling. It uses the available CSS viewport, including Windows display scaling and window chrome, and scales through 4K. Smaller than desktop widths stack the results below the editors.

Trainer navigation sits above the Trainer card in the calculator toolbar. The Player, Trainer, and Damage results cards share aligned top borders and matching outer spacing, keeping both team strips and their common editor rows aligned.

In doubles, drag a team Pokémon into either slot on its own side. Player slots accept the current player team, including its cards in the Team drawer; Trainer slots accept the current trainer team. A new Pokémon replaces the target slot's occupant. Dragging an already assigned Pokémon from its team sprite or slot card to the other slot swaps the assignments. Valid targets highlight without changing the layout, and swaps preserve the currently edited Pokémon's values.

Open the trainer name field and type to search by trainer or location. Group headings show the location or battle name. Results use pages sized to the visible window, with Previous/Next result controls and Page Up/Page Down keys instead of a scrolling list. Selecting a trainer retains the existing team loading behavior; Escape cancels the search.

Stats & details stays visible inside each Pokémon editor at every window size, including while Team & Boxes is open. At larger usable sizes, all four doubles damage tables are visible; compact windows use Slot 1 / Slot 2 to switch result tables. Choosing a slot or damage result also selects its detailed result. Forms, stat stages, and input values survive resizing.

Stats & details keeps Type and Gender together when space allows, followed by optional form and generation controls. Hidden form controls take no space. Aligned numeric columns, consistent field sizes, compact spacing, and subtle row separators keep the table readable; narrower windows wrap complete control groups.

Damage results place the selected move's description and primary damage rolls above the move choices. Additional hit combinations expand through More damage rolls. Each move list is headed by its Pokémon's name, with slot numbers retained in doubles. Selected team sprites keep the same border and fill as their hover highlight, and the highlight follows the Pokémon currently being edited when switching slots.

Battlefield expands upward beside Team & Boxes, with Player and Trainer effects aligned beneath their calculator columns and shared weather, terrain, and field effects alongside them. Each side retains its own hazards, screens, support effects, and Spikes controls. An Active indicator identifies selected effects. Reset Battlefield clears manual effects and reapplies starting weather and terrain from the current matchup's abilities while preserving the selected Pokemon, battle format, and level preference. Preferences opens separately. Notes remain available, with Clear Notes in the notes window.

Team & Boxes expands upward from the bottom of the Calculator page. The drawer reserves space beneath the matchup, and the calculator adapts to the remaining height. Both stay interactive: rearranging Pokémon, assigning slots, and changing color coding use the same live controls and update the current matchup. Team keeps animated sprites and visible names. Box, Box 2, Compost, and Trash use small static pixel sprites without visible name labels. Names and details remain available on hover and through accessible labels. Moving a Pokémon between Team and storage automatically switches its presentation. Drag and drop, assignment menus, speed borders, and damage colors remain available.

Team holds up to six Pokémon in one row. Its cards and animated sprites resize together to fill the available width, with names below each sprite. Box, Box 2, Compost, and Trash adapt their rows, columns, and sprite sizes to the available space after display scaling and open drawer tools. Royal Sword displays at least 30 Pokémon per storage page, with more on larger usable layouts. Generations 1 and 2 retain a minimum of 20. Resizing keeps the first Pokémon from the previous page in view when capacity changes. Color updates retain the current page, while changed search matches reset it.

Pokémon context menus open below or above the pointer according to available space and shift inward at the window edges. Their fixed position accounts for workspace scaling without extending the page. Short windows use additional menu columns instead of scrollbars.

Search & sort, Color coding, Manage boxes, and Import / Export open beside the boxes inside the drawer. These panels do not block the calculator. Selecting the same tool again closes its panel. Team & Boxes collapses the drawer; Escape also closes it when focus is inside, returning focus to the toggle. The drawer is hidden on reference pages and retains its state when returning to Calculator.

Finder and color code help open as native modal dialogs, with topic buttons instead of a scrolling body. Closing help returns focus to its opener. Notes has a larger resizable editor, consistent line number sizing, and a footer that stays outside the editing area; its position and resize behavior account for workspace scaling. About closes when the user clicks outside its popup. About, help, Notes, and Settings use the selected theme.

Import / Export in Team & Boxes opens the import tools without leaving the matchup.

Catch logs show eight records per page, keep newly added catches visible, and retain keyboard focus after removing a record. Update release notes use measured pages that preserve all source text. Long editable notes, imported team text, and searchable dropdowns retain normal scrolling.

The profile chooser displays the Royal Sword logo and Creator: Matroskin. The active profile is labelled Currently selected without the filled toggle appearance used by other controls. Attribution and licensing records remain available in the app's documentation.

The layout follows [Windows responsive design guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/layout/responsive-design): adapt to effective window dimensions, reflow controls, and show secondary details when space allows. Controls use consistent heights and spacing within each task.

The shared app shell does not choose mechanics or input policies. Registered profile layouts still run their reversible lifecycle, and the active profile determines generation, EV policy, trainer data, save context, and optional features.

## Settings and run backups

Settings has Updates, Backup, and Artwork tabs, remembers its selected tab, and provides a button to return to the previous app section. Arrow keys, Home, and End navigate the Settings tabs. Updates retains version information, manual checks, paginated release notes, download progress, retry actions, and installation confirmation. Navigating away keeps the current calculator and update state available.

Artwork manages the optional Pokémon illustrations and static and animated sprites. The first launch prompt offers the complete collection or No thanks; declining keeps pictures off and does not prompt again. Download artwork adds missing files, Check artwork verifies the collection for the installed app version, and Pause download keeps completed files. Show Pokémon artwork changes visibility without removing downloads. The collection remains in app data across updates, repairs, and installation over an existing copy. Uninstall removes the current Windows account's downloaded artwork and keeps saved teams, progress, notes, and settings. Run backups and run resets do not include or delete artwork. See [Dex artwork](pokedex.md#official-artwork).

Back Up Run saves a JSON file for the selected profile. It includes custom sets, team and box order, color settings, imported inventory, global and trainer notes, all encounter modes, trainer progress, item pickups, and associated imported trainer packs. Pending notes are saved before the backup is created.

Restore Run validates the file and compatible profile, asks before replacing the run, and saves a recovery copy for Undo Last Restore. Other profiles and global preferences are kept. Restoring a backup made before item pickup tracking clears pickup checks for that restored profile. Desktop uses native file dialogs; browser use downloads and reads the same backup format.

Report a Problem appears after About in the header. It opens a GitHub draft containing the calculator version, current profile, and generation, with space for reproduction steps and expected and actual behavior. The user reviews and submits the report; run data and attachments are not submitted automatically.

## Dex

The [Dex](pokedex.md) follows all 1,025 National numbers and searches Pokémon by name, form, or number. Details use the selected ROM hack's personal data, move compatibility, evolution rules, and encounter guide. Form controls, stat bars and totals, three labeled ability slots, and official illustration artwork share the main detail view. Further sections cover breeding, training, type defenses, move details, and locations. Missing profile records and unavailable artwork are identified explicitly. Mega buttons switch between Base and supported Mega forms, including separate X and Y variants, within one National entry.

Artwork comes from the public PokéAPI image repository. On desktop, pictures download on first use and are checked and cached for later use. Cards size to their contents, labels align with values, and stats and abilities remain beside a bounded picture. The directory measures its actual remaining viewport for page sizing. The overview reflows with window size and display scaling. Search and form choices remain separate for each profile during the session.

Within Moves, separate Level up, TM, TR, Tutor, and Egg subtabs keep learnsets apart. Move search and page controls apply to the active subtab. The selected method persists while navigating within that profile, and each method has its own empty state when no records are available.

## Profile reference sections

Each reference section has search, filters, a selectable location or trainer list, and a detail view. Arrow keys, Home, and End navigate the section tabs. A profile without bundled reference data displays an empty state instead of another profile's tables.

Reset Run appears beside Encounter rules and in the Trainers and Route Items headers. Each button resets the same progress for the current profile: all encounter records across Normal, Royalocke, and Nuzlocke, excluded families, used or closed locations, item pickup checks, and trainer Beaten checks. A warning explains the scope and requires Accept or Decline. Accept permanently clears that progress without creating an undo copy. Teams, boxes, imported inventory, notes, settings, and other profiles remain intact. The desktop app saves the reset in one operation before refreshing all three sections.

Reference lists use rows sized to their content with separate name and metadata lines. Names wrap inside their selection target. Previous/Next controls and a page selector replace the nested scrolling lists, with page sizes calculated from the available window space. The selected entry and catch controls retain keyboard focus after updates.

Details use the same paging pattern. Trainer teams and route items share aligned card columns; encounter tables split between rows when a compact window cannot display a whole method. Every catch control keeps its original identity and odds continue to use the full encounter method, including entries on other pages. Trainer stats appear directly beneath the moves in six aligned columns. Source evidence and location notes open in dialogs. This follows [Carbon's guidance on readable sizing, spacious data placement and pagination](https://www.carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/core/components/data-table/guidelines).

Encounters keeps its mode tabs and catch actions in the heading, with a compact filter bar and location count in the sidebar. The selected area's name, catch progress, shared area, and closure control sit together above the tables. Encounter rules opens the current mode's explanation and weather guidance. Tables show a common level range once beside the method name; mixed or unlisted levels remain in individual rows.

In areas with shared weather encounters, the first seven land slots appear once in Land (no weather). Weather cards show only their three replacement slots and stay together with Land. On wide screens, Land occupies the first column, weather tables share the middle two columns, and Fishing followed by Shaking Trees occupies the right column. Each block starts at the top, so a tall Fishing table does not get pushed to another page by the weather tables. Tables use consistent 32 pixel rows, with a 24 pixel fallback for shorter windows, following [Carbon's table sizing](https://www.carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/core/components/data-table/guidelines) and [grid spacing](https://www.carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/foundations/2x-grid/overview) guidance. Available odds still use all ten slots. Filtering to a single weather shows its complete table, including the shared slots; search keeps matching shared encounters visible. Smaller windows retain paging without adding scrollbars. Page controls disappear when all encounter tables fit together.

Route Items follows the location order in Encounters, placing additional item locations beside the relevant routes. Items available only after becoming Champion appear together in Post Game at the end of the location list, with their original locations and source links retained on their cards. Search and acquisition filters preserve that order. Every item card has a pickup checkbox that saves separately for each profile and crosses out the checked title. Cards with the same item name remain independent, and a card listing several items has one checkbox for that group. When every item in a location is checked, its name is crossed out in the location list and detail heading. Completion always counts the full location, including items hidden by a search or acquisition filter, and unchecking any item restores the name. Acquisition tags stay at the top right while long names wrap. Pickup progress is included in run backups.

The Royal Sword reference includes:

- Encounters grouped by location, method, and weather, with Normal, Royalocke, and Nuzlocke tabs. Each mode has its own saved progress and reset. Normal allows unlimited catches, including repeats of the same species or evolution family. Its checkboxes record catches, show quantities, and remove one record at a time; the catch log adds further copies. Normal never closes a location or adjusts its base odds.
- Royalocke allows six catches across each of eight Wild Area pairs, and six in Lake of Outrage. City of Motostoke allows one fishing encounter and the Aron trade; Town of Hulbury allows one fishing encounter and the Cottonee trade. Route 2 gifts, Route 5's Togepi gift, and Galar Mine No. 2's static encounter share their location's single allowance with its other encounters. Other locations allow one catch. Closing either paired area early closes both, and reopening either clears both manual overrides.
- Nuzlocke allows exactly one encounter per individual location. Gifts, trades, statics, and starters consume that location's same allowance. There are no shared Wild Area allowances or extra city trades, and manual catches require a location.
- In Royalocke and Nuzlocke, caught evolution families are crossed out and disabled everywhere else, including manual catch log submissions. Missed/used locations do not exclude a family. Exhausted allowances prevent further catches while recorded catches remain removable. A family stays excluded until its last saved catch is removed. Available odds renormalize the full method table after family exclusions; search does not change them. Exhausted allowances, manually closed locations, and special encounters have no available random rate. Weather tables combine the first seven land slots with the three replacement slots.
- Trainer cards include a sprite when artwork is enabled, levels, held items, abilities, natures, moves, and all six recorded stats inline. Sprites prefer saved animations, with matching static forms when an animation is unavailable. Display names and regional/form variants have explicit sprite mappings. A persistent Beaten checkbox beside the battle format strikes out the trainer in the left list; unchecking restores it. Battle notes remain visible separately from the name. Browsing these tables does not replace the active calculator team or trainer packs.
- Trainer rewards show money and awarded items separately from held items. No verified reward data is currently bundled, so both fields say Not recorded. A trainer can supply `rewards: {money: 12500, items: [{name: "Rare Candy", quantity: 2}]}`; money `0` and an empty item array explicitly mean zero and no items. Missing fields remain unknown.
- A Sword/Shield 1.3.2 base game item guide covering pickups, gifts, shops, and berry trees. It is not a verified Royal Sword placement list. Edition limits, location corrections, uncertain placements, match explanations, and source links remain attached to the entries. A placement match does not confirm placement in the game, and stored pool weights must not be interpreted as runtime odds.

Bundled reference assets are keyed by the exact profile ID in `window.KMCalculatorReferenceData`. Their fields are rendered as text, not HTML. New datasets must include reviewed provenance and redistribution permission; importing a trainer pack does not implicitly grant it reference tables or executable UI code.

Encounter progress is saved separately for each exact profile ID and mode through the app's storage bridge. Existing runs load into Royalocke only; the other modes start independently. The last selected mode is remembered per profile. Bundled data contains no personal run progress. Royal Sword supplies its own evolution family registry, including branched evolutions and regional forms, plus explicit aliases for differing encounter spellings. Species absent from the registry use exact species matching. Royalocke catch log locations can be left unassigned for a record for an evolution family; Normal permits unassigned records, and Nuzlocke requires a location.

Royalocke allowances are declared in each profile's reference data. Existing saved catches are retained, including those above a newly applied limit. Older records are matched to their allowance from their species and encounter method; unmatched records remain in the catch log with a review message and still exclude their family. Profiles without explicit Royalocke allowance rules use one catch per location and do not inherit Royal Sword's paired areas. Trainer Beaten progress is saved separately per profile and is independent of encounter modes.
